Plan beyond the square feet

A 3BHK budget is shaped by what each room needs.

A 3BHK may include a master bedroom, two additional bedrooms, multiple bathrooms, a larger living and dining area and more storage requirements. But the final investment depends on the design scope, material grades, furniture quantities and existing site condition — not bedroom count alone.

A clear room-wise brief is the best starting point for a useful estimate.

Start with a room-by-room brief.

List what each bedroom needs, whether all rooms require wardrobes and beds, what is planned for the kitchen and living area, and whether civil, electrical, ceiling and painting works are included.

Compare proposals

Look closely at material and quantity differences.

  • Compare the same furniture dimensions and quantities
  • Verify plywood grade, thickness and brand
  • Check laminate, finish and edge-band specifications
  • Confirm hardware brands and internal accessories
  • Review civil, electrical, painting and ceiling scope
  • Check warranty, payment stages and exclusions
Keep the project controlled

Document approvals before execution.

For a larger home, small scope differences can add up. Extra drawers, premium hardware, changes after design approval, concealed site conditions and additional civil work should be identified and priced transparently.

Ask for written approval of variations before additional work begins. Also consider a contingency for unforeseen conditions and purchases outside the interior contract.
FAQs

3BHK interior design cost — common questions.

Is a 3BHK always more expensive than a 2BHK?

Often the scope is larger, but not automatically. Furniture quantities, material choices, room usage and civil requirements determine the actual estimate.

Should all three bedrooms have the same furniture?

Not necessarily. Each room can be planned for its intended use, such as a guest room, children’s room, study or work-from-home space.

Can I complete a 3BHK interior in phases?

Phasing may be possible. Plan dependencies carefully, especially electrical, ceiling, painting and furniture installation, to avoid rework and extra costs.

What details are needed for an accurate estimate?

Share the floor plan, carpet area, site photographs, room-wise furniture needs, material preferences and whether civil and service work is required. A site visit and measurements may be needed.
